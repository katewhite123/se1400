What make a good photo? Well here's the thing: I could write all about rule of thirds, perspective, exposure, and every other element that makes up a photo, but I believe that you don't have to know a thing about photography to capture a good photo. If you want to learn more about these things, check out the rest of this website! But overall, photography is an art, which means a "good photo" means something different for everyone. If you like the pictures you take, then they become good photos. I enjoyed taking these featured photos because they are all so different and each one taught me something about photography, and something about myself. As I have learned more and more about photography as an art, I have fallen in love with the way color and light can be captured, and I felt these photos showcase light and color in very interesting ways.

Timeless Trail - The trail winds ahead like an easy conversation, familiar and unhurried. Every step feels like catching up with an old friend.

Reflection Lake - The lake rests quietly, reflecting the morning sky like a perfect mirror. For a moment, the world feels completely still.

Golden Good-bye - The sky lights up in a burst of color, like the day’s way of ending on a high note. It feels warm, bright, and impossible not to smile at.

Orange Radiance - Bright and full of life, the flower seems to soak up every bit of sunlight. It’s a small reminder of how something simple can feel so vibrant.

Quiet Growth - The stems fill the frame, creating a quiet world of their own. It feels calm, like a moment to slow down and just exist

Electric Blue - Blue light, soft yet intense, shapes everything it touches. The color becomes the entire mood

Photo Tip - Decide your photo's mood and lighting

Light can make or break a photograph, so train your eye to notice its direction and quality before you even lift the camera. Soft, diffused light—like on an overcast day or during golden hour—creates gentle shadows and flattering tones, while harsh midday sun can produce strong contrasts and unflattering highlights. Try positioning your subject so light falls from the side or behind to add depth and dimension. Backlighting, in particular, can create a beautiful glow or silhouette effect. As you practice, experiment at different times of day and observe how light transforms the same scene in subtle but powerful ways.
